How it works

From a song file to a karaoke night in four steps

Vocal Split is one app for the whole journey. Every step keeps its result, so you never do the slow part twice.

01 · SeparateTake the song apart

Open an MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A or OGG file. The AI pulls the vocal away from the music — or splits drums, bass and the rest as well.

02 · TimePlace the words

Paste the lyrics. Vocal Split finds when every line — and, with the aligner, every word — is sung. Drag anything that needs correcting.

03 · MakeWrite the karaoke

An MP4 video for any TV, in 720p, 1080p, 4K or phone shape, or an MP3+G for karaoke players and hosts' software.

04 · SingRun the party

Queue your singers, put the words on the television, change the key for each person, and let the next song start by itself.

Inside the app
01 · Splitter

Separate vocals and music with studio-grade AI

The Splitter page is where every song starts. It uses Demucs, the open AI model that musicians and researchers use for source separation, running entirely on your computer and sped up with Intel OpenVINO.

Choosing and auditioning the song

  • Choose file Opens MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A and OGG files. You can also drag a file onto the app or use Windows' “Open with”. The card shows the name, format, size and length.
  • Waveform The whole song is drawn as a waveform. Click anywhere to jump there; drag across it to mark just the part you want to separate. The chosen stretch is highlighted and the times are shown underneath (for example “1:20 – 1:50 selected”).
  • ▶ / ❚❚ ■ Every player in the app has the same pair: the round button plays and pauses (pausing keeps your place), the square button stops and goes back to the start of the selection.
  • Set start Set end Reset Mark by ear instead of by eye: press Set start and Set end while the song plays to drop the edges exactly where you hear them; Reset goes back to the whole track.

The Output panel: VU meters and a 15-band analyser

Below the player sits an Output panel modelled on classic studio hardware: a left and right VU needle and, between them, a 15-column spectrum analyser with falling peaks. It shows what actually leaves the app — after the equalizer and effects — and it tells you what is playing (“Output · Vocals”).

  • + − above and below every column move that equalizer band by 1.5 dB. Hold Shift to push it all the way; right-click a column to set it flat. For a moment after each press the analyser shows the whole curve you have set, so you see the shape rather than one band.

Four ways to separate

  • Vocals & Instrumental Both tracks, separated — the voice on its own and everything else together. The one you need for karaoke.
  • Vocals Only Just the voice, for remixes, covers and a cappella.
  • Instrumental Only Just the backing track.
  • All Four Parts Pro Vocals, drums, bass and other — plus the instrumental — for musicians who want to play along, practise or remix.

Processing

  • Start Processing Starts the separation of the marked part (or the whole song). While it works the button fills up like a progress bar.
  • Processing Engine A ring with the percentage, the estimated time left, what is being done right now (“Separating audio into stems”, “Clearing the bleed and the noise left in the stems”) and a Cancel button that stops at once and leaves nothing half-written behind.

Results

Every stem arrives as its own card in its own colour — purple for vocals, green for the instrumental, rose for drums, amber for bass, sky blue for the rest and orange for the trimmed original — so you can tell them apart at a glance. The header says how long the separation took.

  • Player Play, pause and stop each stem, with its own waveform and time.
  • Save Writes the stem where you choose, with a sensible name like “Song (Vocals).mp3” — through your equalizer and effects if you want (see Settings).
  • ⋮ Save as… pick another name or folder · Show in folder opens Explorer on the file · Load as source opens that stem on the Splitter so you can separate it again.

The engine panel

  • AI Model High Quality (Recommended) — the default Demucs model, included with the app and working without internet. Best Quality (slower) — a fine-tuned model with roughly four times the work for a small gain. Fast (quantised) — quicker and lighter, with a little more bleed. The last two download once, the first time you choose them.
  • Acceleration The status card tells you what the separation runs on. On Intel and AMD processors Vocal Split uses OpenVINO, which in our measurements separates about 1.4–1.5× faster than the standard engine with identical results.
  • Output Format MP3 320 kbps or 192 kbps, WAV 16-bit or 24-bit, or lossless FLAC.
02 · Cleanup

A cleaner voice and a cleaner backing track

Every AI separation leaves a little of the music in the vocal and a little of the voice in the music. Vocal Split adds its own cleanup pass after the model: it compares the two stems moment by moment and takes out the bleed and the hiss, while leaving everything the model was sure about alone.

  • Light (recommended) Removes the bleed and the fizz from both stems without touching what matters.
  • Strong Judges each moment harder: cleaner silences, at the risk of thinning a quiet breath or a reverb tail.
  • Off Writes the stems exactly as the model produced them.
03 · Equalizer

A 15-band equalizer with a live response curve

Fifteen bands from 25 Hz to 16 kHz, two thirds of an octave apart, each from −12 to +12 dB. Mud at 250 Hz, boxiness at 400 Hz, presence at 2.5 kHz and air at 10 kHz each get a fader of their own. The equalizer applies to everything the app plays — and, if you like, to everything it saves.

  • Frequency response The curve on top is drawn from the very same maths as the filter, so what you see is exactly what you hear.
  • Faders Drag a fader, turn the mouse wheel over it, double-click to return it to zero, and hold Shift for tenths of a decibel.
  • Equalizer on/off switch — compare with and without in one click.
  • Preset Nine ready curves: Flat, Vocal lift, Warm, Bright, Air, De-mud, Bass boost, Loudness, Podcast — plus any you save yourself.
  • Save preset Delete Reset all bands Keep a curve under your own name, remove it, or start again from flat. Your presets and wherever the faders were left come back the next time you open the app.
04 · Effects · Key & tempo

Space, width, fades — and any song in the singer's key

The Effects rack runs after the equalizer, in the order shown by the lit signal path at the top: Equalizer → Gain → Width → Reverb → Fade. Stages that are doing nothing show “bypass”, so you always know what is active.

  • Output gain Louder or quieter overall. A loud chain is limited cleanly at full scale rather than allowed to distort.
  • Stereo width From mono to extra-wide. Above 100% pushes the sides out.
  • Reverb mix · Room size From a small room to a cathedral.
  • Fade in · Fade out Measured from the ends of the stem, so they land in the same place whether you are listening or saving.
  • Preset Dry, Room, Plate, Hall, Cathedral, Wide, Mono, Vocal air, Instrumental bed, Top and tail — every preset sets every control, so nothing is left over from the last one.
  • Save preset Delete Reset effects and the Effects on/off switch.

Key and tempo

Move anything you listen to up to six semitones up or down and play it at 80–120% of its speed, with a high-quality time-stretch that keeps voices natural. The Original button puts both back. This changes listening only; the karaoke page has its own key and tempo for the video (see below). A track takes a moment to move the first time, then every key you have tried is kept ready.

05 · Karaoke maker

Turn any separation into a karaoke video

The Karaoke page puts the words over the instrumental as a video for your television — every word lighting up as it is sung, with a countdown before each verse, the next line waiting underneath and a progress bar along the bottom. The result is a standard MP4 (H.264) that plays from a USB stick on practically any TV.

The song and its words

  • Which separation Choose any separation from History that has both a vocal and an instrumental.
  • Song Artist Guessed from the file name — track numbers, underscores and website tags are cleaned away — and shown on the title card at the start of the video. Correct them if the guess is wrong.
  • The words Paste the lyrics, one line per line, exactly as they should appear on screen. The counter shows how many lines there are. Import… reads a .txt file, or an .lrc file which already carries its timing.
  • Duets Put who sings on a line of its own before their lines — [Anna], [Mark], or [Both] for lines sung together. Headings like Anna: and [Anna & Mark] are understood too, while [Chorus] or [x2] are simply ignored. Each singer gets a colour of their own in the video.
  • Latin letters under the words Off, Automatic, Bulgarian, Serbian or Russian. Adds a smaller line in Latin letters under every Cyrillic line — perfect for friends and family abroad who speak the language but don't read the alphabet. It follows the official transliteration of each language (Щастие → Shtastie, Ђурђевдан → Đurđevdan) and lights up word by word together with the main line. Automatic recognises the language from the letters used.
  • Missing lines If some lines in the box are never sung on the recording (a chorus written once too often, for example), Vocal Split tells you and leaves them out rather than showing them over the music. Your text is never changed.
Any language and any script works — the words are drawn by the same professional subtitle engine that video players use, so Arabic, Hebrew, Hindi or Thai are joined and ordered correctly.
06 · Timing

Words that land exactly when they are sung

  • Match the words to the voice Uses a forced aligner — an AI that listens to the vocal and finds every word. It rarely needs correcting, and a verse typed once but sung five times is recognised everywhere it comes back, so the box only needs the words, not every repeat. It is a little slower, and needs a 1.2 GB model the first time, downloaded once and kept. With it off, lines are placed on the singing by fast arithmetic.
  • Stop after the timing, to check it first On: the big button finds the timing and stops, so you can check it before writing the video. Off: timing and video in one go. The choice is remembered.
  • The timeline The vocal's waveform, coloured by who sings: the first singer purple, the second blue, both together green. Click a line to see it in the preview; drag a divider to move where a line starts; right-click a line to change who sings it (first → second → both). Corrections are kept with the song, so the next video starts from them.
  • ▶ ■ Play the instrumental — with the guide vocal, key and tempo you chose — and the preview follows along.
  • Preview A real frame of the finished video, in its real look and shape, redrawn instantly as you click or drag.
  • Make Karaoke Video Asks where to save and writes the video. The button becomes a progress bar that says which step it is on (“Step 1 of 2: finding the timing”, “Step 2 of 2: writing the video”) and how long is left. Afterwards it offers to open the folder.
  • Save as MP3+G… Pro Writes the same song as an .mp3 and a .cdg for karaoke players (see MP3+G).
07 · Video settings

Size, background, key and guide vocal

The right-hand column of the Karaoke page holds everything about how the video comes out.

  • Size 1080p (1920 × 1080) for any modern TV · 4K (3840 × 2160) Pro for big screens and projectors, with the words drawn at full resolution · 720p for older sets · Vertical (1080 × 1920) for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, with its own layout rather than a squashed landscape video.
  • Behind the words Four calm gradient backgrounds — Midnight, Ink, Ember, Forest.
  • Use a picture… Put a photo or the album artwork behind the words. Vocal Split can also use the sleeve already embedded in the music file. The picture is darkened only where the words fall, and only as much as that picture needs, so the lyrics stay readable over any image.
  • Count-in before each entry A 3 – 2 – 1 countdown before a line that follows a long pause, so nobody has to guess when to come in.
  • Key Tempo The singer's key (±6 semitones) and speed (80–120%) for this song. The music in the video is moved and the words keep up with it. Play above to hear it first; the choice is kept with the song.
  • Guide vocal From Off up to −6 dB: the original voice left in quietly, for anyone who does not know the tune — just like the machines in karaoke bars.
  • Preset Save preset Delete The size, count-in, guide vocal and look, kept together under a name for next time.
  • Encoder A card showing which H.264 encoder your computer will use — graphics card (NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync, AMD AMF) or Windows' own — tested on your machine, not just listed.

Make the video yours

Choose a ready-made look, or design your own — the typeface, the colours, the weight, where the lines sit, how big they are, and your logo in a corner.

  • Look Six ready looks: Classic, Gold, Neon, Stage, Karaoke machine, Soft. Each sets the typeface, colours and weight, and leaves your placement, size and logo alone.
  • Typeface Pro Any font installed on your computer — checked, so a video never silently falls back to another font.
  • Sung Waiting Ground Pro The colour of words once sung, before they are sung, and the edge or box behind them. Second singer, in a duet sets the second voice's colour; both singers together get the two mixed.
  • Readability check The card shows the contrast of your colours (for example “Reads at 5.4:1 sung and 16.9:1 waiting”) and warns you if a choice would be hard to read.
  • Bold Where the lines sit Size of the words Pro Heavy or regular type; lines higher, in the middle or lower (to keep a face in the picture clear, or stay away from TV overlays); words from 80% to 130%.
  • Add a logo… Pro Your bar's or channel's logo in any corner of every frame, with its size and transparency. A PNG with a transparent background looks best.

10 · MP3+G Pro

MP3+G for karaoke players and hosts

MP3+G (CD+G) is the format of karaoke discs and of the software karaoke hosts use every night — software that can change the key live. Vocal Split writes a pair of files with the same name: Song.mp3 and Song.cdg.

  • Built for the format's limits A CD+G screen is 300 × 216 pixels with 16 colours, redrawn a little at a time. Vocal Split keeps the two lines in fixed places and sends the colour sweep first, so words light up on time — measured at 0.06 s late on average and 0.14 s at worst across a whole song.
  • Duets and colours Each singer's colour comes from your look.
  • In step to the sample The MP3 is written with the encoder's usual silent lead-in removed, so the words never run early.
  • Same audio as the video Guide vocal, key and tempo included.
11 · Karaoke party

A karaoke night on your television

The Party page turns your laptop into a karaoke machine. Build a queue of singers and songs, put the words on the TV, and let Vocal Split move from one singer to the next.

The queue

  • Add from History Any separated song whose words are timed. It is drawn live on the TV in your Karaoke page look — with count-in, duet colours and Latin letters.
  • Add files… Ready karaoke videos (MP4, MKV, MOV, AVI, WEBM, M4V) and MP3+G pairs from any library — pick either the .mp3 or the .cdg.
  • Every row A box for who sings, the song and its kind, the singer's key with − +, ↑ ↓ to reorder, and ✕ to remove. Every kind of song can change key — even ready videos and MP3+G.
  • Remove all Clears the queue. The queue is kept when you close the app, so a party set up in the afternoon is still there in the evening.

Now playing

  • Up next / Singing now Who sings what, the progress through the song and the key. The next song is prepared in advance — key change included — so it starts the instant you press Start.
  • Start: name → Pause → Resume One big button for the whole night.
  • Restart From the top · Stop ends the song and puts it back at the front, not sung · Skip moves on · − + changes the key while the song plays, without stopping.
  • Sung tonight A running list of who sang what.

The television

  • Screen Open the screen Connect the TV as a second screen and the words fill it while the queue stays on your laptop. With one screen, the words cover it and the keyboard runs the party.
  • Between songs Wait for Start, or start the next song by itself after 5, 10 or 20 seconds.
  • Smooth playback The TV picture runs at a steady 25 frames per second in 1080p, drawn separately from the sound so a busy screen never interrupts the music.

12 · Batch Pro

A whole folder of songs, done overnight

The Batch page works through a queue of tracks one after another — separating each, and making the karaoke video and MP3+G for every song that has its words beside it. Start it in the evening and find a finished library in the morning.

  • Add tracks… Add a folder… Single files, or a whole folder with all its subfolders. Files the app itself wrote are left out automatically, so a folder that contains the last batch's output is never fed back in.
  • Words by name Put Song.txt or Song.lrc in the same folder as Song.mp3. Each row shows whether words were found (“words: Song.txt” or “no words beside it — stems only”).
  • Status Every row says Waiting, Working, Done, Skipped, Failed or Stopped, with the current step, a progress bar and what came of it. Waiting rows can be removed with ✕.
  • Clear finished Remove all Start Batch / Stop Batch Stopping waits for a safe moment and leaves nothing half-written; Start then offers to try the stopped and failed tracks again. One failed song never stops the rest.
  • For the whole batch Its own Extraction (including All four parts), AI model, Output format and Cleanup, kept apart from the Splitter's, plus the Output folder with Choose….
  • Make karaoke videos For every song with its words, using the Karaoke page's size, background, count-in, guide vocal and look.
  • Make MP3+G files An .mp3 and a .cdg for every song with its words — with or without the videos.
  • Match the words to the voice The forced aligner, as on the Karaoke page.
  • Name the files “Artist - Song” For a library sorted by artist: “01-artist-song (www.site.com).mp3” becomes “Artist - Song”, and the folder, stems, video and MP3+G all take that name.
  • Skip tracks already done Anything already in the output folder is left alone, so a stopped batch — or a folder with a few new songs — picks up where the last one finished.

13 · Mixer Pro

Mix the parts — and your own recordings — back together

The Mixer page puts every part of a separation on its own line, with a level, a place between the speakers, a mute and a solo. Add a track of your own — a take you sang, a backing track from another source, a guide — and Vocal Split lines it up with the song by listening to it: where it starts, its speed and its key. Everything is heard as you set it, and the result is saved as one file.

The song and the transport

  • Which separation Any separation in History — vocals and instrumental, a single part, or all four parts. For a four-part separation the lines are Vocals, Drums, Bass and Other; the instrumental is left out, since it is those three added together.
  • ▶ / ❚❚ ■ Play, pause and stop the mix. The waveform above the tracks is the mix as it is set — click anywhere on it, or on any track's own waveform, to jump there.
  • Live Nothing is mixed ahead of time: a mute, a level or a nudge is heard within a fortieth of a second, without the song stopping, and a change of level is ramped so a mute never clicks. The mix plays through the equalizer and effects, at the song's own key and speed.

Every track

  • Level From off up to +6 dB, in half-decibel steps, with the value beside it. Drag it to the bottom for off; double-click to put it back to 0 dB.
  • Pan From left to right, with the position shown as L, C or R and a number. Double-click for the middle.
  • Waveform Each track drawn on the song's own timeline, all to one scale — so you see where an added take starts, and how loud each part is next to the others.
  • M Mute — the track stays in the list but is silent. A muted track is drawn dimmer, sliders and all.
  • S Solo — only the tracks marked S play. Several can be soloed at once.
  • ✕ Takes an added track out of the mix. The file you added is never touched.

Quick mixes

  • Everything No vocals Vocals only No drums No bass Drums and bass No other Which parts play, in one press — offered according to the parts the separation has. Levels and your own tracks are left as they are. Practise the bass line with the bass taken out, sing over the band, or hear just the rhythm section.
  • Reset levels Every track back to 0 dB, in the middle, unmuted and not soloed.

Your own tracks, lined up automatically

Add a track… takes MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, AAC, OGG, Opus, WMA and AIFF files, at any sample rate — several at once if you like. Each is compared with the song and with every one of its parts, and placed against the one it agrees with most: a sung take against the vocal, a backing track against the instrumental. It works in the background, and the track shows Lining it up with the song… until it is done.

  • Where it starts A longer intro, a count-in, a track that begins in the middle of the song — found to the millisecond. In our tests the error was under 4 ms.
  • Its speed A version played faster or slower — from about 16% slower to 19% faster — is brought to the song's speed.
  • Its key Sped up the way a tape runs, key and all, or made in another key — up to six semitones away, which is how backing tracks are often made for a singer — and it is brought back to the song's key.
  • Sure or not The rhythm proposes where it goes, and the notes decide: two unrelated songs at the same tempo line their beats up, but not their notes. A track that does not sound like the song is left at the start, with a note saying so, for you to place by ear.
  • What was done Written under the track's name — for example “its first 1.454 s come before the song · sped up 3.1%” — and in full on the Your track card, with the part it was matched against.

The Your track card

  • Choose a track Click an added track in the list to bring it to the card.
  • −100 ms −10 ms +10 ms +100 ms Move it earlier or later by hand, heard at once — the card says how far it has been moved.
  • Line it up again Forgets the hand moves and listens once more.

Saving and keeping

  • Save the Mix Everything that is switched on, at its levels and places, as one file — MP3, WAV or FLAC, in the format chosen in Settings, and through the equalizer and effects when Settings says so. A mix that would clip is brought down as a whole rather than distorted. The line under the button says what is in the mix.
  • Kept with the song The levels, mutes and added tracks are remembered with the separation, so the mix is as you left it next time. The lined-up copy of an added track is kept in the separation's folder and removed with it.
  • From History A separation's ⋮ menu has Open in the mixer.
14 · History & cutting

Every separation kept — and cut to length

The History page keeps your recent separations, so you can play and save their stems again without waiting for the AI a second time.

  • Open / Close Shows the stems of a run as cards, with play, stop, waveform, Save and their own ⋮ menu.
  • ⋮ Make karaoke video opens the run on the Karaoke page · Open in the mixer Pro · Show in folder · Delete this run.
  • Clear all Deletes every kept run from this computer, after asking.
  • Output meters The same VU needles and analyser as on the Splitter, with the +/− band buttons.

Cut

  • Mark a stretch On the stems mixed back together — drag the waveform, or use Set start Set end while it plays; Reset starts again.
  • Keep only the selection / Take out the selection Trim a long intro and outro, or remove a solo and join the two sides.
  • Fade at the cut From none to 2 seconds — a gentle fade when a stretch is kept, an equal-power crossfade when one is taken out. The panel says how long the result will be.
  • Hear the result Save as new run Listen first, then save. Every stem is cut at exactly the same sample, so vocal and instrumental stay in step for karaoke, and the original run is never touched.

Under the hood

Built carefully, measured honestly

  • Demucs AI The hybrid-transformer separation model (htdemucs), included with the app so it works on the first run with no download.
  • OpenVINO acceleration On Intel and AMD processors, 1.4–1.5× faster than the standard engine, with identical output.
  • Forced alignment A speech model with a commercial-friendly licence finds each word in the vocal; its thresholds were tuned on real Bulgarian and Serbian songs.
  • High-quality time-stretch Signalsmith Stretch moves key and tempo while keeping voices natural.
  • Automatic track alignment Onsets and notes compared at every speed in range: an added track's start, speed and key are found without being asked for.
  • Professional text rendering Lyrics are drawn by libass with HarfBuzz and FriBidi — correct for right-to-left and complex scripts.
  • Hardware video encoding H.264 through your graphics card or Windows itself, in colours labelled BT.709 so TVs show exactly what you chose.
  • Fast writing A 4-minute 1080p video is written in about 1 minute 20 seconds on a two-core laptop; 4K takes about three times as long.
  • Any audio file Formats the audio library does not read, such as M4A, are decoded by the FFmpeg built into the app.
  • Protected, native code The app's own code is compiled to native modules.

Your music stays on your computer

Vocal Split never uploads your audio, your lyrics or anything about what you do. Separations, settings and presets are kept in your own Windows profile, and Settings has a button that deletes all of it. The only time the app reaches the internet is to download an optional model you asked for, or when you click a link to this website.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does Vocal Split work without an internet connection?

Yes. Separation with the default model, karaoke videos, MP3+G, the party and everything else work offline. Only the two optional AI models and the word aligner are downloaded — once, when you first choose them.

Is my music uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens on your own computer. There is no account, no cloud processing and no tracking of what you separate.

Which files can I open?

MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A and OGG for separation. The Party page also plays karaoke videos (MP4, MKV, MOV, AVI, WEBM, M4V) and MP3+G files, and the Mixer (Pro) also takes AAC, Opus, WMA and AIFF tracks.

Can I add my own recording to a separated song?

Yes, in the Pro Edition's Mixer. Add a take you sang, a backing track or a guide, and it is lined up with the song automatically — its start, its speed and its key — then mixed with the parts you choose and saved as one file. If it does not sound enough like the song to be placed with confidence, it is left at the start for you to move by hand in 10 or 100 ms steps.

How good is the separation?

Vocal Split uses Demucs, one of the best open separation models available, followed by its own cleanup pass that removes most of the bleed between voice and music. Results depend on the recording — heavily reverberant or very dense mixes are always harder — but for karaoke the instrumental is usually clean enough that nobody notices anything is missing.

Where do the lyrics come from?

You paste them — or import a .txt or .lrc file. Vocal Split deliberately does not download lyrics from the internet: you stay in control of the words and of the rights to use them.

What if the timing is wrong in places?

Drag the dividers on the vocal's waveform to move where a line starts, right-click to change the singer, and watch the preview update instantly. Turn on “Stop after the timing” to check everything before the video is written. Your corrections are kept with the song.

Will the video play on my TV?

Videos are standard MP4 files with H.264 video and AAC sound — the combination practically every TV, media player and USB stick setup accepts. Choose 720p for very old sets, 1080p for most, and 4K for big screens and projectors.

Can I make karaoke in languages other than English?

Yes — any language and any alphabet. For Bulgarian, Serbian and Russian, Vocal Split can also add the lyrics in Latin letters under the Cyrillic line.

How do duets work?

Write who sings on a line of its own before their lines — for example [Anna], [Mark] or [Both]. Each singer's words light up in their own colour, and lines sung together in a mix of both. You can also right-click any line on the timeline to change who sings it.

What is MP3+G and do I need it?

MP3+G is the classic karaoke format used by karaoke discs, players and hosts' software (which can often change the key live). If you only play karaoke at home from a USB stick, the MP4 video is all you need. If you run karaoke nights with dedicated software, MP3+G (in the Pro Edition) fits straight into your library.

Can I use Vocal Split for my karaoke business or YouTube channel?

Commercial use needs the Pro Edition licence. Please note that the licence covers the software; the rights to the music you process are your own responsibility.

How does the karaoke party work with one screen?

The words fill the whole screen and the keyboard runs the party: Space starts and pauses, → skips, ← restarts, ↑ ↓ change the key, and Esc brings you back to the app. With a TV connected as a second screen, the words go to the TV while you manage the queue on the laptop.

How long does separation take?

It depends on your processor. On a modest two-core laptop a 4-minute song takes around 14 minutes; faster processors take proportionally less. You can mark just the part you need, and a batch can run overnight.

What does the free edition include?

The whole app on the first 60 seconds of any song, up to three songs a day — including karaoke videos and the party — so you can judge the quality for yourself before buying.

Is Vocal Split available for Mac or Linux?

Not at the moment — Vocal Split is made for Windows 10 and 11.

What language is the app in?

The interface is in English. The installer is available in fourteen languages.
